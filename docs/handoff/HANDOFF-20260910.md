## 1. THE ONE THING THAT MATTERS FROM THIS SESSION

**The port had regressed to 1.3 frames of visible motion per second and
no instrument caught it for eight days.** Mike caught it by playing:
"since you agreed on an arch to move in, I haven't gotten a single build
from you that has moved frames."

Cause: `FBXPORT` (added 2026-09-08 so the port renders on the MiSTer at
all) makes the line single-buffered. The master composes into the bank
being displayed, so a new picture appears only when a whole compose
finishes. The framebuffer flipped 9 times in 2988 vints.

Why it hid: every ranking instrument adopted since 2026-09-01 measures
the 68K's LOGIC. `gameplay_speed.py` reads 49.7% on a rom whose picture
is frozen. `START-HERE.md`, written 01:23 that same morning, declared
that number the only bar and explicitly ruled out "flip rate... screen
updates per second". An overnight loop then ranked four hours of builds
on it. LOOP28 93 had ALREADY measured this divergence and been overruled
by Mike's play pass; the next document made the gate the only bar anyway.

**New instrument: `tools/presented_fps.py`.** Over 60 consecutive ares
frames it counts frames differing from their predecessor by >5% of the
active area (MOTION, the headline) and by >0.25% (any-change). Verified
deterministic: two runs of the same rom agree to the digit.

**`START-HERE.md` is corrected**: the bar is now stated as 60 fps
REACHING THE SCREEN, with `presented_fps.py` MOTION as the ranking metric
and `gameplay_speed.py` as a precondition, not a substitute.

## 3. THE PALETTE QUESTION IS CLOSED — DO NOT REOPEN WITHOUT NEW PHYSICS

This consumed most of the session and ended in a proof, not a
measurement. **The supply side is exhausted.**

    The Mega Drive has FOUR CRAM palette lines, 16 colours each, shared
    globally between planes and sprites.
      line 0    the text grey ramp + the MDSPR sprite anchor
      lines 1-3 the background colour pack (m_main.c:288)

    The 68K uploads the sprite palette as a 15-word DMA to CRAM offset 2
    (md_main.c mdspr_consume, 0x930F + 0xC002) -- LINE 0 ONLY.

**Can the background fit in two lines, freeing one for sprites?
NO, and this is settled by exhaustive search.** `tools/mdpen_bake.py`
partitions colour sets across lines requiring each line's union of
quantised colours <= 15 pens, and fails loudly rather than falling back.
Its line count is now parameterised (env `MDPEN_LINES`):

    MDPEN_LINES=3  normal: 39 sets, 36 colours -> lines [15,15,11]
                   (4 pens spare)                            BAKES
    MDPEN_LINES=2  BAKE FAIL: 39 sets / 36 colours have no exact 2x15
                   partition -- a real capacity limit

36 colours do not fit 30 pens. Arithmetic, not tuning.

**What the decompile thread contributed, and it was the better half of
this work:**

  - Sharing is not a lever: 12 real palettes need 123 distinct colours at
    once; the tightest pair union is 20 against 15 pens; zero pairs fit.
  - **Counting live sprite RECORDS instead of distinct colour sets
    changes the picture**: the top 3 palettes by record count cover 65-75%
    of everything drawn, with no colour change at all. That metric is the
    right one and it overturned my "6 sets, 3 lines, hopeless".
  - The palette table is derived, not guessed: source 0x242A0 + 28*index,
    14 words; destination 0x840800 + 32*slot + 2, pen 0 transparent.
    168 of 176 entries distinct.
  - `$0A` is the RUNTIME slot (0-63, written by the allocator at 0x3B6C);
    `$0B` is the COMPILE-TIME identity (0-0xAE). See section 7 for my
    error here.

**But the 70% figure describes a DEMAND we cannot SUPPLY.** Three sprite
lines would leave the background one, which does not partition. The
decompile thread's finding is correct and unreachable on this hardware.

**What IS banked from it:** `MDSPRTOP=1` (LOOP29 132). The scene table
pinned the normal scene's anchor to set 0x09, which carries 3 records,
while set 0 carries 7 — line 0 was spent on the wrong palette. The flag
runs the existing leader election in every scene (it was gated to the
wildcard boss scene) and switches on a 2-record margin held 5 passes
instead of only when the incumbent owns nothing.

    MDSPR_WHY census, level-1, ~600 vints:
                        without   with
      CLAIMED             477      569    +19%
      NO BAKED KEY       1313     1204
      palette mismatch    890     1076
      records examined   2785     2931
    both runs reconcile: reasons sum to records examined.

**`MDSPRTOP` has NOT had a play pass.** LOOP29 130 measured the winning
palette trio changing once in 19 consecutive frames and four times in 13
samples spaced 100 frames apart, so an anchor switch is an event rather
than churn and the exposure is small — but nobody has looked at one.

Also banked: the sprite art bake was covering ONE colour set. `SCENES` in
`tools/bake_mdspr.py` said `(0x09,)` while its own docstring claimed
0x00/0x10/0x0B. Widened to `(0x09, 0x0A, 0x0B)`: keys 8 -> 16, art 6944B
-> 9120B, inside the 12288B VRAM window, nothing dropped. That moved the
claim census from 75.2% NO BAKED KEY to 33.5%, with the rejections
landing on palette mismatch instead — two gates in series.

## 4. WHAT WAS RETRACTED, AND WHY — READ THIS BEFORE TRUSTING ANY ENTRY

Four substantive claims died this session. Every one had the same shape:
**a correctly measured quantity, followed by an inference about machinery
I had not read.** Three were caught before any code was written; one was
caught only after building it and looking at the screen.

### 4a. "The game's palette refcount table tells us which sets need a
hardware pair" (LOOP29 114, corrected by 115)

The allocator at 0x3B2E/0x3BCE is real, its tables at 0xFFF440/0xFFF480/
0xFFF500 are real and live in our work RAM, and our sprite key `d[4]&0x3F`
IS the game's slot index (81 live slots over 10 frames, one disagreement).
All verified. But a refcount counts OBJECT references, including actors
that are off-screen and drawing nothing: slot 6 had refcount 1 and ZERO
on-screen records at f2000. Not claiming it was correct.

### 4b. "The 20-record MDSPR cap is the constraint" (LOOP29 119)

It is not. Claims ran at 0.83/generation against a cap of 20 and an MD
hardware capacity of 80 — 1.3% of the chip. The constraint was the art
bake (75.2% NO BAKED KEY). Caught by measuring instead of assuming.

### 4c. "The background's third palette line is free" (LOOP29 121,
superseded by 133)

I read MD CRAM at ONE level-1 frame, counted 30 distinct colours, and
concluded 30 fits 30 pens. The pen bake counts 36 over 180 classified
samples. **A single frame is not a scene.** I then built `BGPACK2=1`, saw
the sky go black, and misattributed it twice — first to the packer being
unable to dedupe (it does dedupe; `mdp_assign_set` is a greedy best-fit
with cross-line colour reuse), then to the baked `mds_s_line` table still
handing out line 2 (that IS a real bug, m_main.c:1921, one constant with
two sources of truth). The actual reason is that no 2-line partition
exists at all.

### 4d. "The two framebuffer banks diverge by 88%" (LOOP29 131,
retracted by 135, and its hypothesis in 134 with it)

**The framebuffer bank is 128 KB, not 64.** `packet_fmt.h:170` puts the
packet at SH-2 offset 0x12000, so a bank cannot be 64 KB. I dumped
0x20000 of 32X DRAM and split it in half, comparing the top and bottom of
a SINGLE framebuffer — which differ because they are different parts of
the same picture. Correct stride:

    bank0 f700->f1000  8667 of 131072 bytes changed
    bank1 f700->f1000  7283            <- both active, neither frozen
    bank0 vs bank1     6583  (5.0%)    <- two different frames
    packet region        60 of 2048

Both banks are live and a 5% difference is what double buffering should
look like. There is no incoherency defect. **The decompile thread's
LOOP-DECOMPILE 10-12 figure (266 of 40960, one bank byte-identical across
300 frames) should be re-checked against the 0x20000 stride** — if they
used 64 KB the same artifact applies.

## 5. THE OPEN QUESTIONS, RANKED

### 5.1 Where do FLIPEDGEOFF's 12 points go? — ANSWERED 2026-09-10, LOOP29 136

**Measured with `--trace-flip` and the 68K stamp trace: the master's FS
write lands at active line ~26, the hardware defers the latch to the
next vblank (ares vdp.cpp:95-97; MiSTer VDP.sv:400 and the FBCR readback
at :165 behave the same), and the master then spins on the latch with
FM STILL RAISED (m_main.c ~6188) for a median 198 lines. The game's text
writer spins on FM to the end of the frame, the frame overruns, and the
next vint's entry is late: 33 of 33 deferred-flip vints are followed by
a late entry, miss rate +16.7 points in the window.** Every accepted
flip costs the game the rest of that frame. The paragraph below is the
question as it stood; the answer and the candidate split are in LOOP29
136.


Dropping the vblank edge guard buys all the motion (3.7 -> 16.7) and
costs 12 points of game speed (47.1 -> 34.9). Flipping should be free —
the MiSTer RTL defers a late FS write rather than tearing
(`srcref/S32X_MiSTer rtl/32X/VDP.sv`, LOOP28 91). My bank-incoherency
explanation is retracted (4d).

**MEASURE IT, do not hypothesise.** `tools/stage_lines.py` reads the
shipping build's own `PSTAMP` V-counter stamps — no probe code to get
wrong — and split the 68K shim cleanly this morning. Run it with and
without the flag and compare stage lines. Three hypotheses died on
measurement today; this one should start there.

### 5.2 CAT1MD — the largest lever not blocked by hardware

Cat1 tiles are 0.67 vints/generation, **48% of the SATURATED processor's
compose**. `CAT1MD=1` already moves them to MD plane A. It passed the
still-image gate on 2026-09-07 and FAILED Mike's play pass for shimmer
and a second palette on the ground band through the transform. That is a
RENDERING DEFECT with a knowable cause, not a capacity wall.

I asked the decompile thread for a fifth question on exactly this: how
does the game decide a tile is category 1? If the port classifies
heuristically where the game has a rule, that is the likely root of the
shimmer.

### 5.3 The Plane B name-table bug — the one open correctness defect

START-HERE's open bug: the attract title never renders. The name table is
computed correctly in SDRAM (341 -> 4 distinct BG slots at the cut), MD
Plane A follows it (148 -> 2), Plane B does not (338 -> 207).

**The decompile thread narrowed this usefully (LOOP-DECOMPILE 10-12):**
Plane B always draws tile page 0, Plane A always page 7; the whole
program writes the page-select registers at exactly two sites with the
same constants and there is no register-indirect writer anywhere. **So
the 4 KB to instrument is 32X DRAM 0x12000**, the page scr2 reads from.
They also tested and killed their own hypothesis (a bank flip landing
between the unpacker's two sweeps) rather than reporting it.

### 5.4 Lower value, all real

  - `MDSPRTOP` needs a play pass (+19% claims, built, unvalidated).
  - The sprite bake pads to bounding rects: 1610 of 7479 tiles (21.5%)
    are fully blank. Worth ~21%, but VRAM is at 74% so it buys nothing
    until the offload grows. Indexed tile SHARING does NOT pay: dedup

    express, 12% at 4 tiles, 0.7% at 16) because each animation frame is
    distinct art.
  - Shadow-ramp draws under load: SPRLATE[3] = 1099 on opt1, the red
    rectangle at f4000.
  - `BGPACK2`'s baked-table bug (m_main.c:1921) is real and unfixed, even
    though the flag it was blocking is now known to be a dead end.
  - The round-clear beam phase (from an earlier session).
  - The audio arc has not started at all: a software YM2151 on the SH-2

## 7. THE FAILURE PATTERN — THE REASON THIS THREAD WAS ENDED

Stated plainly, because it is the most transferable thing here.

**The instruments were mostly fine. The inferences were not.** Every
retraction in section 4 has the same structure: measure a real quantity
correctly, then assume machinery that was never read. The refcount table
existed and did not mean what I said. The cap existed and was not the
constraint. The 30 colours were real and were one frame of a scene. The
banks were real and I used the wrong stride.

**Specific self-inflicted defects worth knowing about:**

  - Counters inserted between `if (cond)` and `continue;` **without
    braces** made five `continue`s unconditional and silently disabled
    the MDSPR offload entirely. The census then read plausibly and was
    measuring itself. Caught only by checking that the rejection reasons
    summed to the records examined. **A probe that edits control flow
    must reconcile its own totals before one number is believed.**
  - A `static uint32_t` array nothing READS is dead-store eliminated by
    `-O2 -flto`: 8 of 10 counters vanished and read back zero. **Probe
    counters must be `volatile`.** Same class as the `pal_streak`
    counter that was inert for weeks (LOOP28 85).
  - I divided per-generation work by the CYCLE count, producing a phantom
    3.5x discrepancy between two correct instruments. `DIAG[9]` counts
    595 cycles where `NAT_WALL[1]` counts 167 generations. START-HERE's
    own rule 4 is "divide before you claim".
  - I put a WRONG FACT in the decompile brief: that `$0A palette_bank` is
    a compile-time constant, citing `move.w #$5D,palette_bank(a6)`. That
    is a WORD write at offset $0A — 0x00 into $0A, 0x5D into $0B. I had
    verified the OFFSET (`clrb %fp@(10)`) and never checked the WIDTH.
    The decompile thread caught it; anyone enumerating $0A writes was
    enumerating the wrong field.
  - I narrated time from the MiSTer's log clock instead of git, after my
    own memory note says never to do that.
  - My first `sdram_map.py` parsed neighbouring comment prose and
    announced `pri_lut` as 256 KB — nineteen fake overlaps. My first
    `flag_audit.py` inert-state scan reported three writes-that-exist as
    inert (nested indices, `&name[...]` pointers, macro aliases). **Tools
    that cry wolf are worse than no tools**; both were rewritten
    conservatively.
  - The overnight loop died after 28 minutes of a 6-hour night because
    `ScheduleWakeup` was the LAST step of a tick and one long tick never
    reached it. Fixed in `docs/handoff/LOOP-NIGHT-0909.md`: schedule
    FIRST, and cap exploratory ticks.
  - I twice left a PROBE BUILD in `rom/s16.32x`, the shipping path. The
    `rom/night/` directory and `tools/night_run.py` exist precisely so
    probe output does not land there.

**What actually worked, and should be the default method:**

  - Reading the shipping build's OWN stamps (`stage_lines.py` over
    `PSTAMP`) rather than adding probe code — nothing to get wrong.
  - Asking a tool that does exhaustive search and fails loudly
    (`mdpen_bake.py --lines 2`) instead of sampling. Three sampling-based
    attempts got the palette question wrong; one parameter on the right
    instrument settled it in two minutes.
  - Reconciling totals. It caught the brace bug and validated every
    census afterwards.
  - Mike's play pass. It caught the FBXPORT regression that eight days of
    instrumentation missed, and it correctly called dblfast as "more
    frames, no speed" before the logic numbers were run.

Running against `srcref/alteredbeast` (Michael J Archer's commented

`docs/handoff/HANDOFF-DECOMPILE.md`, which also carries replies from this

All four ranked questions answered:

  1. Palette demand — sharing is not a lever (section 3).
  2. Tilemap unpacker — narrows the Plane B bug to 32X DRAM 0x12000
     (section 5.3).
  3. **Sprite-RAM writers — the best structural result.** Exactly ONE
     writer in the whole program, a loop at 0x2B16 inside IRQ4, with both
     inputs in work RAM the shim can already read (a 256-entry order list
